# F1: What do scaling-law studies (Kaplan 2020, Chinchilla, follow-ups) say about parameter-count vs coherence in the 10M–300M range, and what is the empirically established minimum viable size for a decoder-only autoregressive transformer to produce readable English?

## Findings

### [1] Scaling laws predict a smooth power-law improvement in test loss with parameter count, spanning 7+ orders of magnitude with no observed lower bound
- quote: "The loss scales as a power-law with model size, dataset size, and the amount of compute used for training, with some trends spanning more than seven orders of magnitude."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361
- source_type: primary
- published: 2020-01-23
- confidence: high

### [2] Kaplan et al. fit L(N) = (Nc/N)^0.076, meaning doubling parameters yields ~5% loss reduction — the trend is monotonic and shows no phase transition or "coherence threshold" in the scaling curve
- quote: "L(N) = (Nc/N)^αN; αN ∼ 0.076, Nc ∼ 8.8 × 10^13 (non-embedding parameters)"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361
- source_type: primary
- published: 2020-01-23
- confidence: high

### [3] Kaplan et al.'s smallest trained models had ~768 non-embedding parameters (shape (2,128)), and even these followed the power law — the paper does not identify a minimum viable size for coherence
- quote: "Model size (ranging in size from 768 to 1.5 billion non-embedding parameters)"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361
- source_type: primary
- published: 2020-01-23
- confidence: high

### [4] Chinchilla (Hoffmann et al. 2022) found compute-optimal training requires model size and training tokens to scale equally (~20 tokens per parameter), training 400+ models from 70M to 16B parameters — but the paper addresses compute-optimal allocation, not minimum coherent size
- quote: "for compute-optimal training, the model size and the number of training tokens should be scaled equally: for every doubling of model size the number of training tokens should also be doubled."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556
- source_type: primary
- published: 2022-03-29
- confidence: high

### [5] TinyStories (Eldan & Li, 2023) demonstrated that models with below 10M parameters trained on simplified vocabulary (~1500 words) can produce fluent, grammatically correct multi-paragraph stories — but this is on a constrained domain, not general English
- quote: "we show that TinyStories can be used to train and evaluate LMs that are much smaller than the state-of-the-art models (below 10 million total parameters), or have much simpler architectures (with only one transformer block), yet still produce fluent and consistent stories"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-05-12
- confidence: high

### [6] TinyStories explicitly states that 125M-parameter models (GPT-2 small, GPT-Neo small) trained on general corpora (Pile, Common Crawl) "can rarely generate coherent and consistent English text beyond a few words even after extensive training"
- quote: "Models with around 125M parameters such as GPT-Neo (small) or GPT-2 (small) can rarely generate coherent and consistent English text beyond a few words even after extensive training."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-05-12
- confidence: high

### [7] TinyStories found that on their simplified dataset, coherence (not just grammar) requires hidden dimension ≥128 and ≥2 layers — a 1-layer model with 21M params maintained grammar but failed context-tracking entirely
- quote: "the ability to generate a completion that is consistent with the beginning of the story emerges when the hidden size of the model increases from 64 to 128."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-05-12
- confidence: high

### [8] TinyStories found that grammar plateaus early and can be mastered by small models, but consistency and creativity require larger scale — implying "coherence" is not a single threshold but a gradient of capabilities
- quote: "while grammar can be mastered by relatively small models, consistency and creativity only emerge at a larger size."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-05-12
- confidence: high

### [9] The scaling law exponent αN ≈ 0.076 implies that going from 10M to 300M parameters (30x) reduces loss by factor 30^0.076 ≈ 1.30 (23% reduction) — significant but not transformative, confirming no sharp phase transition
- quote: "L(N) = (Nc/N)^αN; αN ∼ 0.076"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361
- source_type: primary
- published: 2020-01-23
- confidence: high

### [10] The practical minimum for readable general-domain English from the evidence: ~100M+ parameters on web-scale data, though this threshold is highly dataset-dependent — on narrow/synthetic data, sub-10M can suffice
- quote: "Could it be that SLMs are overwhelmed by the amount and variety of information they have to process and store, and that this hinders their ability to learn the core mechanisms and principles of language?"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-05-12
- confidence: medium
