# F5: What perplexity/cross-entropy loss values correspond to 'readable' English in GPT-style models? Is there any published loss-to-human-judgment calibration or empirical threshold (e.g., 'models below X nats/token produce garbage, above Y produce coherent text')?

## Findings

### [1] Cross-entropy of ~3 nats/token produces the highest human-rated text quality (fluency, coherence, overall quality)
- quote: "texts that had cross-entropy rate τ=3 received the best ratings by human participants for fluency, coherence, and overall quality. Further, for τ=3, more than half of raters mistakenly guessed the AI-generated text to be human generated."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14966v2
- source_type: primary (peer-reviewed paper with human evaluation experiments)
- published: 2020-07-29, revised 2021-01-14
- confidence: high

### [2] Below cross-entropy ~2.5 nats/token, text enters the "boredom trap" with excessive repetition
- quote: "for small values of k and p, both top-k and top-p sampling methods fall into low cross-entropy regions—boredom traps—which results in increase in repetitions as the length of the generated text increases"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14966v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2020-07-29
- confidence: high

### [3] Above cross-entropy ~5 nats/token, text enters the "confusion trap" with increasing incoherence
- quote: "for high values of k and p in top-k and top-p sampling methods respectively, the observed cross-entropy of the generated texts increases with the length of the generated texts. This leads to increase in incoherence in the text as the token index increases—the confusion trap"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14966v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2020-07-29
- confidence: high

### [4] Human-written text has a cross-entropy of ~5.13 nats/token when scored by GPT-2
- quote: "Human | 5.13" (Table 3: Human generated data for human evaluations)
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14966v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2020-07-29
- confidence: high

### [5] Sentence-level repetitions disappear above cross-entropy ~2.5 nats/token for GPT-2
- quote: "for larger values of n, the percentage n-gram repetitions is very close to zero for cross-entropy greater than 3. This indicates sentence-level repetitions disappear after a threshold of cross-entropy whereas word-level repetitions continue to appear for larger values of cross-entropy. Also, note that... we do expect a good sampling algorithm to have minimum sentence-level repetitions, which all the sampling seems to show beyond a threshold of cross-entropy, which seems to be around 2.5 for GPT-2."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14966v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2020-07-29
- confidence: high

### [6] Cross-entropy has a near-linear relation with repetition, mostly independent of sampling method
- quote: "repetitions vary heavily with observed cross-entropy rate in the generated text and mostly independent of sampling method used"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14966v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2020-07-29
- confidence: high

### [7] GPT-2 (124M) achieves val loss ~3.12 nats/token on OpenWebText; GPT-2 XL (1.5B) achieves ~2.54
- quote: "gpt2 | 124M | 3.11 | 3.12 ... gpt2-xl | 1558M | 2.56 | 2.54" (nanoGPT baselines table)
- url: https://github.com/karpathy/nanoGPT
- source_type: primary (official training reproduction)
- published: 2022-2025 (ongoing)
- confidence: high

### [8] GPT-2 124M fine-tuned on OpenWebText reaches val loss ~2.85 nats/token
- quote: "taking the GPT-2 (124M) checkpoint and finetuning on OWT directly for a while reaches loss down to ~2.85. This then becomes the more appropriate baseline w.r.t. reproduction."
- url: https://github.com/karpathy/nanoGPT
- source_type: primary
- published: 2022-2025
- confidence: high

### [9] Low generative perplexity does NOT guarantee text quality — zero-parameter samplers can achieve "SOTA" gen-PPL while producing incoherent text
- quote: "we construct a suite of zero-parameter, deliberately naive samplers that achieve state-of-the-art gen-PPL on LM1B and OpenWebText at non-degenerate entropy, surpassing recently published diffusion and continuous-flow models while producing text that is incoherent by construction."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08417v1
- source_type: primary (peer-reviewed workshop paper)
- published: 2026-06-07
- confidence: high

### [10] Periodic repetition of top tokens achieves gen-PPL of 29.4 on LM1B (lower than real models) while being completely incoherent
- quote: "Periodic k=64 | 4.16 | 29.4 | 0.004 | 435.0 | 128.144 | 0.000 | 0.500 | 0.496 | 0.491" (Table 1, periodic sampler achieves gen-PPL 29.4 vs. MDLM's 83.8)
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08417v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-06-07
- confidence: high

### [11] OpenWebText reference corpus has gen-PPL of 17.2 under gpt2-large (corresponding to ~2.84 nats/token)
- quote: "Reference | OWT train | 5.48 | 17.2 | 1.000 | 0.0 | 0.000 | 0.482 | 0.437 | 0.083 | 0.026"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08417v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-06-07
- confidence: high

### [12] No single hard threshold exists — quality is a function of both model size and cross-entropy range
- The Mirostat paper demonstrates a "sweet spot" at ~3 nats/token for GPT-2 (117M), but this is sampling cross-entropy (how predictable the generated text is to the model), not training loss (how well the model fits the data). These are related but distinct quantities. A model with higher training loss can still produce high-quality text if sampling is controlled well.
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14966v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2020
- confidence: high

## Suggested follow-ups
- What is the relationship between training loss and sampling cross-entropy for small (<300M) models specifically? Does a 30M model need to reach the same loss as GPT-2 124M to produce readable text?
- For small models trained on FineWeb-Edu, what validation loss ranges have practitioners reported as producing "readable but not great" text in community benchmarks (e.g., llm.c, nanoGPT forks)?
- How does tokenization choice (BPE vocab size) affect the mapping between nats/token loss and perceived text quality?
